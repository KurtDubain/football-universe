Pacing is the second of the six changes shipped together in this round. The live modes gave every event-free minute the same 540 ms of the same passing loop, so a half with fourteen fouls in it looked identical to a half with none, and the commentary feed repeated the scoreline for most of the match. `buildRoutineTimeline` closes that gap out of the simulation's own numbers rather than by invention: it subtracts what the event feed already accounts for from each authoritative `MatchStats` total — fouls, corners, shots, shots on target — and places whatever is left on the clock, capped at 62% of minutes so quiet stretches still exist and still read as quiet. An own goal is charged to the side the simulator credits it to, because `simulator.ts` reconciles shots on target as goals + key saves + blocks per side; a save or a block is charged to the other side. Nothing in the stream can touch the score, the stats or the event feed — a routine beat is something to watch and something to say, never something to count. Each kind now holds the screen for its own length (corner 960 ms, foul 880, save 820, off-target shot 680) while a silent minute drops from 540 to 430, so the match runs on two gears instead of one. A foul is the one beat that stops play: `stoppage.ts` stages the whistle, pins the ball, and pulls only the players near enough to have an opinion into a ring around the referee, releasing them before the free kick. A throw-in joins the restart vocabulary as presentation texture — the simulator does not tally throw-ins, so the renderer may use one to explain how possession restarted but never reports it as something that happened. Highlights mode is untouched, because it exists to skip exactly these minutes.

Ball height is the third of the six changes shipped together in this round, and it is the one that answers the report that the ball flies and that transitions feel abrupt. Pass height was a step function of distance: `longBall = travel > 0.34` chose the arc, so a short pass topped out at 0.18 arc — 0.44 of a drawn player — and the next metre of travel started at 0.55 and ran to 0.95, or 2.32 players, two bands that did not overlap at all, with 31–47% of passes crossing the line depending on pattern. `counter` was the worst of the six patterns at a 95th-percentile arc of 0.923, which is exactly the transition the report named, and an interception triggered at 55% of flight — the apex — then snapped the ball to the grass from as high as 2.29 players. Height is now a property of the pass rather than of its length: `PATTERN_ARC_CEILING` gives each pattern a ceiling (counter 0.24, central_combination 0.28, recycle 0.3, wing_overload 0.7, build_up 0.9, switch_play 0.95) and distance climbs towards it on a squared ramp between 0.14 and 0.40 of the pitch, over a floor of 0.055 and a ±18% roll. `openPlayDuration` was made continuous in the same pass, because the old code had two bands (42–62 and 70–95 frames) either side of the same threshold, a metre of distance moving the median from 52 frames to 82. A ball cut out mid-flight now keeps its remaining height and decays it at 0.86 per frame above a 0.4 floor, in `decayBallLiftCarry`, so it settles instead of dropping. Measured over 11,314 phases from 4,000 seeds: a 44 m counter peaks at 0.69 of a player and a 44 m switch of play at 1.91, where the two were identical before; a 70 m build-up ball peaks at 2.38; the highest interception fell from 2.29 players to 0.41. The interception trigger itself stays at 55% of flight — with the arcs this much lower the residual decay covers it.
